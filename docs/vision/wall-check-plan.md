# Periodic wall check while exploring (plan, 2026-10-09; recommended, not yet built beyond the dry run)

Goal (user): G2 looks often enough to notice a wall coming, not so often that constant pictures slow everything down. No Claude API call is involved; everything runs on the Pi.

## What exists
- `vision/wall_distance.py`: finds the floor/obstacle boundary row in five vertical strips, converts it to centimetres with a tape calibration (`Calibration`, box at 20/30/40/60/100 cm), says "clear", "blocked" or "nearest N cm, would turn left/right" (near threshold 30 cm).
- `explore_session.py`: the wall estimator reads the interest watch's cheap peek (a camera snapshot that is assessed, not saved, every `G2_INTEREST_EVERY_S`, default 10 s) and only LOGS to `~/.local/share/g2/wall_dryrun.jsonl` (`G2_WALL_DRYRUN`, default on). It never moves G2. The vision avoider is unwired until the user says.

## Recommendation
**Look once per leg, at the stand between legs, and shorten the next leg to what was seen. Nothing continuous.**
1. **When:** at the end of every walk leg while G2 is standing still (7 s legs, so a look about every 8-10 s of motion), plus once at the start of the session and after every turn. A still body gives a sharp picture and the stand already exists, so the look costs no extra stop. A peek is a camera grab plus a few milliseconds of image maths; the picture is not saved.
2. **Why this rate:** at the exploration speed (about 0.05-0.10 m/s) one leg covers 35-70 cm. A look per leg means he never walks farther than he last saw. The current 10 s peek timer is close, but it is not tied to the legs (it can fire mid-stride, blurred).
3. **Leg length from distance:** next leg seconds = min(default 7 s, (nearest cm - 25 cm margin) / speed). Clear beyond 100 cm: full leg. Nearest under about 40 cm: turn toward the open side (the estimator already names it) before walking. Under about 20 cm or "blocked": no forward leg, turn in place, look again.
4. **Cost control:** no look while sleeping, resting or during a picture stop (the survey picture counts as a look); never faster than once per 3 s; the last 20 estimates are kept in memory and only the log line per look is written (a flagged "turn" case also saves its jpeg to a small ring of 10 for review).
5. **Failure handling:** two bad grabs in a row (camera error) keeps the current behavior (short legs of 3 s) and logs it; the wall logic must never block the safe stop.

## Calibration from pictures (2026-10-10)
The first calibration was built from labelled recognition pictures, not by moving a box: `python -m pi_pipeline.vision.wall_distance pictures ~/g2_wall_pics shot_007 shot_008 shot_009 shot_010 shot_011` (the same median-of-the-middle-three-groups rule as `calibrate`; `calibrate_from_pictures` / `check_against_pictures` in `wall_distance.py`). Pictures were taken with G2 standing, wall straight on, at 8, 12, 16, 24 and 40 in; the second pass (picked up and re-placed each time) repeated within 3 px, and a simple lens model (floor line = 115 + 608 / distance in inches, in a 240 px picture) fits to 2 px. Limits: rows come in steps of 1/24 of the picture (10 px), so between 8 and 24 in the reading is good to about 2 to 4 in, and beyond 24 in it only tells far from near (24 to 40 in differ by 14 px). Pictures stay on the Pi (`~/g2_wall_pics`), never in the repo.

## Wall and detection logging (2026-10-10)
Every wall look in an exploration session is one line in `~/.local/share/g2/wall_dryrun.jsonl`: time, state (`clear` / `far` / `near` = nearest wall base at or inside 12 in / `blocked`), nearest distance in inches and per column group, the turn it would make, the exploration mode, and for near / blocked looks a picture name; the pictures are a ring of the newest 20 in `~/.local/share/g2/wall_pics` (plus one clear look every 5 minutes, for comparison). Detections (animals) were already logged per day in `~/g2_runs/detections/`. Read both with `python -m pi_pipeline.vision.wall_stats [--days N]`: looks per state, a histogram of the nearest wall in inches, the turns it would make, the times he stayed near a wall, and how often each detection label was seen. `G2_WALL_DRYRUN=0` turns the wall log off. Nothing here moves G2.

## False near readings and the wall-height rule (2026-10-10)
A wall 10 ft away read as 9 in because a bright sheen on the glossy floor looked like an obstacle base. `base_rows` now takes an obstacle only if it rises at least 6 consecutive grid rows (a quarter of the picture); shorter patches (a sheen, a floor-grain strip) are skipped and the search goes on upward. Checked on the 13 saved pictures: 10 ft now reads 40 in or more ("far"), the 16 in shot that had a floor-grain outlier reads 16 in on all groups, and every wall from 8 to 40 in is unchanged. Still biased: the 6 ft shot reads about 20 in because the wall's own reflection on the floor adds a row or two; beyond 24 in the estimator can only tell far from near. The log also marks `near_confirmed` when two looks in a row are near. A chair or table leg (narrow, under a quarter of the picture tall) is no longer an obstacle for this estimator.

## Prototype picture set 1 and the camera roll (2026-10-10)
The first 13 wall pictures are **prototype set 1** (about 4 deg of camera roll, the floor line 12 px lower on the right than the left on all 11 straight-on shots; shots 1 to 9 overexposed, mean brightness about 172 against a target of about 100; taken through the voice service's picture path, not the detection feed the exploration peeks use). The user decided NOT to correct the roll in software (a fixed correction could throw other readings off) and to retake better pictures in a later session, accepting that left/right turning may be a little off at first; the roll is recorded in the labels file for that session. The calibration built from set 1 is provisional. For the retake: check the exposure before every picture (mean 90 to 130, under 1 percent blown out), take the pictures through the same path the exploration peeks use, and straighten the camera if possible.

## Wall steering (2026-10-10, prototype; the user approved "start steering him so we can experiment")
During an exploration roam (Tier 1, started by voice or by `g2_explore.sh start`) a near wall now turns G2 away: `BehaviorDriver._wall_reflex`. It acts on the newest wall look when it is fresh (under 8 s old) and either `blocked` (a turn of about 80 deg) or `near` (12 in or closer) on two looks in a row (about 50 deg), once per look, toward the side with more room, and it only turns, never walks. A soft two-note falling sound (the "turning away" sound, 70% of the usual level) plays each time so you can tell why he turns. Needs the calibration on the Pi (`wall_calibration.json`); an uncalibrated look is never acted on. While it is on, the peek (and so the wall look) comes every 4 s instead of 10 s. Off switches: `G2_WALL_STEER=0` (no steering, 10 s peeks), `G2_WALL_SOUND=0` (silent turn). It does NOT apply to a plain voice walk ("walk forward") or to a stationary session; those have no wall steering. Every look and every steering decision is in `wall_dryrun.jsonl` / the journal (`g2.wall.steer`); read the statistics with `python -m pi_pipeline.vision.wall_stats`. Known limits (prototype set 1): the left/right bias from the camera roll, readings beyond about 24 in that only say far or near, reflections on glossy floor, and one look every 4 s while he moves at about 4 in/s.

## Steps
1. Calibrate on hardware (hardware plan block 5): `wd.calibrate()`, box at 20/30/40/60/100 cm; check the logged distances against tape within about 5 cm.
2. One dry-run exploration session with the per-leg look wired to the log only; review the log for false positives (table legs, rugs, shadows, floor seams, tile lines, a person standing).
3. On the user's word only: let the leg planner use the estimate (step 3), first in a cleared room with soft barriers, with the user near.
4. Tests (no hardware): fake snapshots with known boundary rows; the leg planner returns the shorter leg and the turn side; camera failure falls back; the safe stop still works.

Open questions: wall seen at an angle (the boundary is slanted: use the nearest strip); glossy floor reflections; what to do at a step-down edge (the floor ends: the same boundary maths reads far away, so edges need their own cue; not covered here).
