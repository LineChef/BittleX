G2 head · hands-free trigger

Head Button Pusher

What you would install so the Pi can press the head's side button. Two ways: push it from outside (nothing soldered), or wire across the switch (one fine solder joint). Nothing here is bought or tested yet.

Option A · push from outside · no soldering, board untouched

What you install

  • A mini push solenoid, 5 V, spring return, body under about 15 mm, stroke 4 to 6 mm, with a flat wide tip (a clear rubber dot, about 1/4 inch, on the tip softens the hit and widens it). A rigid bracket holds it to the shell with the tip 0.5 to 1 mm off the button at rest.
  • A MOSFET driver board (3.3 V logic). A Pi pin cannot drive a solenoid directly.
  • 3 wires to the Pi: a signal pin, 5 V and ground, run along the neck cable.
  • Optional: a 470 µF capacitor across the 5 V feed to absorb the pulse.

The button is a raised cap about 1/40 inch (0.025 in) proud of the shell, in a 1/8 inch oval opening. The shell around it is the stop: a tip wider than the opening pushes the cap in and no further. Unknown until you measure: the cap's height edge-on, the force and travel that trigger the switch, and the solenoid's pull on G2's 5 V supply.

Option B · wire across the switch · one fine solder joint

What you install

  • One thin wire (about 30 AWG) from the switch's signal terminal to a Pi pin.
  • One resistor, 330 Ω to 1 kΩ, in line.
  • Kapton tape to hold the antenna cable aside while soldering.
  • Ground is already shared through the body's cable.

The joint is the risk: the antenna cable, the connector and tiny neighbors are all next to it. A repair shop could do it in minutes.
