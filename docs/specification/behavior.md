# Behavior

The behavior subsystem makes G2 act on its own as well as on request. `BehaviorDriver.tick()` is the one place that composes every autonomous behavior.

Behaviour
---------

- Tick: Each tick reads the current inputs (detections, IMU, voice commands, battery) and returns an ordered list of effects (move, chirp, speak, take a picture). It never touches hardware, and bindings turn effects into real actions, so all autonomy can be tested with no robot.
- Modes: `CONVERSE` (a conversation is active, no autonomous movement), `IDLE` (awake and still), `EXPLORE` (walking exploration) and `APPROACH` (a directed come-here walk).
- Phases: Within the one exploration task the driver reports a phase: look (standing or resting and watching), move (walking) or pause (a survey stop, hit sequence or naming look). The phase is read from the mode and the running choreography, and every change is logged.
- Exploration entry: A roam starts only on a spoken request, or on a yes after G2 asks "Can I go exploring?" following 3 minutes of idling. G2 never starts roaming on his own.
- Wall steering: A near wall turns G2 away toward the side with more room. A wall turned away from three times at the same distance within 20 s hands over to the hit sequence.
- Hit sequence: On a wall hit or an IMU contact signal, G2 backs up and turns around toward the open side.
- Survey stops: G2 stops at times for a claw-look picture, using one shared arm stepping, a 0.8 s settle and a dropped stale frame.
- Ending: Every session ends in the verified rest position, and the roam stops its behavior loop before resting.
- Live follow: `g2attend 1 ball live` follows continuously. A camera thread takes pictures back to back (about 2 to 3 a second) and a control loop steers the claw, ten times a second, to where the target was when the newest picture was taken (the claw's pan at that moment, plus a correction at half the offset), so the picture's age does not make it overshoot. With `stream` (`g2attend 1 ball live stream`) the pictures come from the module's own continuous stream, about 4 a second and steady, instead of one request each. The picture is still about half a second to a second old by use, so it follows a hand-speed target. It keeps the bout's focus lock, fall, sag and stop-file handling, and logs every picture and move.
- Active look (the shared behavior): one function, `behavior/active_look.run_active_look`, that every mode which follows with the claw calls. It raises the arm to 120 degrees (nothing moves if the arm is already up; otherwise he goes into the attention pose first, switch `attention_first`), then follows with the claw on the camera's picture stream using the tracking method it is given. A tracking method is any callable taking a picture (and when it was taken) and returning detections as centre and size in frame fractions; it can be bound to the follow to read the claw's pan and the filter's prediction. The ball's is `behavior/ball_tracker.py` (the colour finder, the ball's own colour, and a motion fallback accepted only within 12 degrees of where the filter expects the ball); another target needs only another small class. An end-of-follow judge decides when it is over and each mode picks its own: time only, the ball's stop-or-lost rule, or a centred rule for a look that is done once the claw has settled on the target. The arm's height is a setting of the call (120 degrees by default). Every use keeps the per-picture log in its result. A ball that the fixed colour rules miss is looked for by its own colour in every look, and that colour (the median of the latest close samples) is remembered between sessions so the first look of a session has it (the chase's looks and the watch's pictures use one shared finder). When the watch's last picture finds nothing it takes up to three more from where the claw stands before the ball counts as lost, and every missed look is noted as numbers, with at most six missed-ball pictures kept per session for review. Before every walk, turn, back-up and punt the tilt is read afresh and a body more than 12 degrees off level is laid down and stood up again, and a stand-up that ends more than 8 degrees off level is repeated once; the pitch and roll each stand-up ended at are logged to the journal. The safety is the same in every mode: a fall, a body that tilts more than 10 degrees from how it stood, and the stop file end the follow whatever the tracking method and judge are, and a caller can lower those limits but never raise them. A tracking filter keeps the target's bearing and speed, so the claw leads a moving target, coasts through pictures with no sighting for up to a second, and holds a sighting more than 30 degrees from the prediction until a second one agrees. The function never walks, takes no still, lowers nothing and stands nothing up: the caller does what comes after. An experimental exposure lock (off by default, always undone) freezes the sensor's auto exposure for the follow. The ball's post-punt watch and the supervised live follow both call it; mode parity rule R8 fails on a second follow loop. The `g2followcal` test measures the claw's delay and pan speed so the gain, lead and coast can be tuned from numbers.
- Active look (the post-punt watch): in the ball goal (setting `post_punt_watch`, on by default) and in `g2punttest`, G2 watches the ball with the claw after a punt instead of taking one picture afterwards. Speech: "Got it." is said as soon as the stream shows the punt was good (the ball ended farther away or moved, by the scorer's own rule; two sightings in a row) or the ball is lost, always before "I lost the ball"; the scorer then does not repeat it, and a failed punt still ends with "Missed." The watch starts standing, right after the punt, and holds until the ball stops or leaves view. When it ends, the claw is centred, G2 lies down in rest and stands up again, and only then does he say target acquired or turn toward where the ball was last seen. The stop detector counts sightings, not consecutive pictures (four agreeing sightings within about 4 s, gaps allowed: a pale ball is seen only now and then), and the stream must lose the ball for 4 s before it counts as lost. Even then one last picture is taken with the claw where it was pointing, and a ball that had stopped there is found. After a loss the goal turns by the last bearing (nothing when it was within 25 degrees of straight ahead, at most 40 degrees otherwise) before the aimed claw look. A stream sighting more than 30 degrees from the last tracked bearing, soon after it, is held until a second sighting near it agrees, and a blob under about 7 px across is not a ball (small false blobs at the frame edges steered the claw). During the follow the stream finder falls back to the ball's own colour (the chase samples it from a reading within 14 inches) when the fixed rules find nothing, the claw aims about half a second ahead of a ball that is moving (the speed of its bearing), and the journal keeps a per-picture log (seen or not, centre and width, brightness, size, and the biggest green blob in a missed picture; numbers only). The ball's place is the picture after the stop, or, when that picture misses it, the median of the stream's last few sightings (size gives the distance). A missed still logs the biggest green blob it held, as numbers only. When a chase loses a ball it had a bearing for, the second empty picture brings one claw look aimed at that bearing before any blind search (setting `directed_look`). In the goal, the result is the attempt's after-punt reading, and the next attempt starts from where the ball stopped, or turns toward where it was last seen. `g2punttest` stands G2 up, punts the ball, and watches it. The camera stream starts at once; he goes to the attention pose and raises the claw; the claw follows the ball until its world bearing (the claw's direction plus the ball's offset in the picture) and size have stayed put for about 1.5 s, or 10 s pass. Then one picture fixes where it is. Found: he says "target acquired" and the chase goes on from that reading (rest, the eased stand-up, a turn toward it, the walk, the claw range, one more punt). Lost: he says "I lost the ball", turns 40 degrees toward the side it was last seen on and does the normal claw look. `g2punttest dry` only watches a ball rolled by hand.
- Camera probes: `g2stream` asks the module for a continuous stream of pictures and reports pictures a second and the gaps, and `g2liftcal` steps the claw lift down from 120 with a ball fixed in view and records the picture's vertical shift and the body tilt at each step, to decide whether the live follow can also tilt the camera. Both are hardware tests. The lift calibration showed the picture does not move when the lift changes (the view is the same at lifts of 120 to 60), so the claw cannot follow a target up and down; only the pan follows it.
- Attentive bout: A supervised bout (`g2attend`, run only with someone watching) holds the attention pose with the claw raised and, for a few minutes, turns the camera toward what the camera model detects (largest first), slowly (a move at most every 1.5 s and only part of the way, because the frames are about a second old). After 10 s of following he only glances, and he looks around when nothing is in view. It stops on a fall, a sag, or the stop file, holds the focus lock, and always ends with the claw down and centred and the standard stand-up. It never walks. The camera model has three classes and no human or face class, so it follows detections of those classes, and with a named target it also finds that object with the object gallery (the recognizer exploration uses to name objects), read-only. The target `ball` is the green ball, found by the ball chase's colour finder. The bout is switched off: it refuses to run unless `G2_ATTENTIVE_BOUT=1` is set, because the active look after a punt replaced it.

Interfaces
----------

- Inputs: Detections, IMU, voice events and sensor readings through the runtime.
- Outputs: Effects to the actuator, the narrator, the chirper and the camera.
- Personality: Composable traits (curiosity, mood, bonds and others) shape both the Claude prompt and the behavior parameters.

Parameters
----------

- `G2_WALL_STEER`, `G2_HIT_WALL` and `G2_IMU_CONTACT`: Turn the wall turn-away, the hit sequence and the IMU contact signal on or off (all on)
- `G2_EXPLORE_INPROCESS`: Runs the exploration inside the voice service
- The 300 s default roam length and the leg budget bound a bout.

See [Exploration mode](../guides/exploration-mode.md) for the full list.

Constraints
-----------

- Ball finding: a colour finder looks for a round green blob in a normal pass, then a loose pass for a big dim blob close to the claw, then dim passes for a pale ball on the floor. A pale ball 24 to 30 in away is only about 230 px and sits at the horizon (rows 75 to 77 of 240), and one 5 to 9 in away is 1000 to 3400 px, so the passes cover those: any detection from a dim pass is confirmed by a second picture. The planner also checks readings for a jump: when the ball read within 8 inches, only a turn or a look has happened since, and the next reading is more than 15 inches farther, it is a different green thing and is treated as the ball having vanished at close range (back up, look again), never walked toward.
- Approach steps: a small step closer is timed to the gap to the ball (aiming to leave it 3 inches from the camera, at least 0.4 s, at most the step length), and one long walk covers at most 60 percent of the distance to the ball, so a timed step that lands long never carries him into it. Settings `nudge_proportional` and `walk_max_fraction`; the step rate is a measured value that the calibration refits.
- Move gate: before every move the chase, the goals and the drills send (walk, turn, back, punt), the body's tilt is read. More than 12 degrees off level on pitch or roll and the move is not sent: G2 lies down in rest and stands up again from rest (never through the attention pose, which is the move that tipped a rolled G2 over), then the move goes. The same check runs before the claw look's attention-pose entry. A tilt past the fall angle is not a sag: he is laid in rest, no stand-up is sent, and the move reports a fall so the session stops. The gate is in the standing flow's stand-up check, so it applies to every mode that moves through it.
- Consistency: A behavior (stand-up, claw look, rest, sag handling, turns, camera use, what a fall is, what a deaf board is) is the same in every mode. It is defined once and the modes only call it: the claw look is the ball chase's sequence (`run_claw_look`), the body limits are in `pi_pipeline/body.py` (a fall is a tilt past 60 degrees for 0.3 s; a board with no motion data for 20 s is deaf), and the guards, the sag recovery and the responses to a fall and to a deaf board are built by `gait/body_guards.py` for every mode. `tools/check_modes.py` fails a commit that builds a second copy, and `docs/specification/mode-exceptions.md` lists the only differences allowed: the ones explicitly requested.
- Claw look and walking: The claw look never starts while a policy walk is running. It waits up to 8 s for the walk to end and, if the walk is still going, is skipped, so the arm and the attention pose never compete with the walking legs.
- Focus lock: While the lock is held (a goal session, an exploration, or `g2focus on`), G2's own initiative stands aside: no "Can I go exploring?" ask, no sit, rest or sleep descent, no idle fidgets and no look-around bouts. Spoken commands, the stop words, the emergency stop, the fall and battery guards and a roam someone asked for are never held back. Each holder expires on its own (a run's hold within 60 s of a crash, a person's within 4 h at most), and every held-back behavior is logged and counted. Every autonomous behavior asks one function, `focus.allow(kind)`.
- Emergency stop outranks everything.

Failure handling
----------------

- A failed behavior tick is skipped and the loop continues.
- A fall during a roam stops the session and rests G2.

Verification
------------

- `python -m pi_pipeline.behavior` runs the driver against mocks, and the tests drive it tick by tick.
- Robot: A supervised roam with a hand near, then `python -m pi_pipeline.diag report`.
