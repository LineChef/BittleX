G2 Wiring Blueprint

Every jumper between the Pi Zero 2 W, BiBoard, PiSugar, the mic/amp pair, and the reserved screen slot — one page to check against before unplugging anything, and to rewire from after.

Overview

This is the real physical stack, not just a wiring hub: PiSugar mounts directly under the Pi today, and the planned screen goes one layer further out, under PiSugar. BiBoard and the mic/amp pair aren't part of that stack — they're jumpered off to the side, mounted elsewhere on the frame.

Wire map

The Pi header is drawn standing up, the way it sits on the board: pin 1 top-left, pin 2 top-right, running down to 39/40. Odd pins are the left column, even pins the right. BiBoard sits on the left, the mic and amp on the right; wires from a far-side pin loop around the end of the header or run through the gap between pin rows.

Notes for future-you

"Fan-out" vs "either pin" — two different things look similar in the table but aren't. GND is flexible: every GND pin on the Pi is the same electrical net, so mic and amp can each use a different free ground pin — nothing forces them onto pin 9 specifically. BCLK/LRCLK are not flexible: the Pi's I2S hardware only drives those clocks out of GPIO18/GPIO19 specifically, and both boards only ever listen — they never drive the line themselves. One driver with two listeners isn't a conflict, but it does mean both boards physically need to reach that exact pin.
How the BCLK/LRCLK split is actually made — hand-built from 3 stock jumper wires per signal, not a purchased splitter (a pre-made 1-pin female-to-2-female splitter exists and would have worked, but wasn't worth a 2-week/$10-shipping order for a 99-cent part) and not soldering directly onto the mic's tiny pads (which would've meant permanently sacrificing the rewireable point of this whole reference) and not stacking two connectors on one pin (mechanically marginal — a standard header pin is ~8-8.5mm exposed, two female socket housings need ~8-10mm combined to seat reliably). The actual build: take a female-to-female wire (keep its female end → Pi's pin), a second female-to-female wire (keep its female end → mic's header pin), and a female-to-male wire (keep its male end → straight into the amp cable's matching wire, which is female — confirmed from the amp's product photos, its cable is 5 separate individual wires, not one molded block). Strip the other end of all three, twist together, solder, insulate with heat-shrink or tape. One joint per signal, away from either board — electrically identical to a factory splitter, and the solder joint holds up to vibration better than a friction-fit one would have anyway.
PiSugar mapping — the 2/4/5/6 pin range is confirmed by PiSugar's own PCB legend and FAQ, but the exact pin-by-pin match isn't independently verified. If the Pi ever fails to power on after reseating PiSugar, orientation is the first thing to check — it bolts on in two 180° rotations and only one lands the pogo pins on the header.
Pin 2 carries two things — PiSugar feeds 5V in here, and the amp draws 5V from the same pin. That's normal — it's a shared power rail, not a conflict — but if you ever unplug the amp's VIN wire, don't read a live pin 2 as "amp still connected."
Amp CTRL (pin 36), soft-resolved — the vendor's docs (github.com/nulllaborg/i2s_audio_amplifier_module) mention a "CTRL pin for level setting," but the amp's own product photos show only the one 5-pin I2S connector — no separate control pin or pad visible anywhere on the board. Leaning toward nothing to wire here; the "smart software tuning" marketing claim likely lives inside the NS4168's own I2S-based logic. Not 100% certain without the datasheet, but non-blocking — the overlay works the same with pin 36 unwired either way.
Mounting — amp: confirmed M4 mounting holes, Lego-compatible, straight from its product photos — a real screw mount, better than adhesive. Mic: no equivalent confirmation yet; foam tape is the fallback if it turns out to have none.
Screen section is a placeholder — pins 11/17/18/19/20/22/23/24 are reserved so nothing else claims them, not because a screen is wired yet. Whatever SPI display you land on, `DIN/CLK/CS/DC/RST/BL` are all software-assignable — this is a suggested layout, not something imposed by the display itself.
